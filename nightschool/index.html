Method

The growth iteration log

Each test is written up before it starts, with a metric, a data source, a baseline, a readout date and a kill bar. Ideas come from data, user behavior and what other growth teams publish. A claim stays a hypothesis until our own numbers move.

On statistical power. At today’s volume most single tests can’t reach significance. So I lean on holdouts, guardrails set before launch, one change per test and kill dates, and I say plainly which results are reproduced and which are not.

Acquisition · AI answers
Diagnose lost questions before writing new pages

Running · rerun after 14 days
Saw
I tracked 24 buyer questions (English, German, Spanish) in Google and AI-assistant answers. We were cited in 0 of 24. Reddit was cited in 22 and YouTube in 17.
Changed
I sorted each lost question by cause before building anything: 11 weak pages, 8 won by third-party sources, 5 indexing issues, 0 missing pages. I then rewrote the 8 weakest pages answer-first in 14 languages. A manual Search Console check showed 3 of the “indexing” pages were indexed after all, so my own proxy was wrong and I corrected the diagnosis.
Measure
Rerun the same 24 questions in Google and two AI answer engines 14 days after release. Secondary metrics: non-branded clicks and signups referred by AI assistants.

Acquisition · search
Kill a test before building it

Deprioritized on data
Saw
The idea was to rewrite titles on locale pages with impressions and a click-through rate under 1%.
Changed
I pulled 710 pages (14,089 impressions). The biggest low-CTR pages were homepages whose queries were the old brand name. That traffic belongs to another company, so rewriting titles can’t win it and shouldn’t try. Only two real candidates were left.
Measure
I logged it as small and moved it below the next tests. One data pull saved a week of work.

Activation
The sign-in leak

Shipped
Saw
Event data showed 78% of visitors who reached sign-in left without trying the product.
Changed
I rerouted the primary buttons to account creation instead of sign-in.
Measure
I measure it on the step-by-step sign-in funnel. Over May to August, the share of signups who finished a first lesson rose from 29% to 51%. That is a cohort trend, not a result I attribute to this one change.

Monetization · checkout
Walk the purchase path as a stranger

Shipped · readout pending
Saw
In 72 hours there were 9 real checkout starts and 0 payments.
Changed
An agent walked the checkout signed out, on desktop, on mobile and in German, without ever paying. It found the payment page called every kit “Build your first kit” and never said what the buyer gets or when. It also found the German page switched currency, and on mobile the Buy button sat below the fold. I shipped a fix that names the kit, says what arrives and recovers abandoned checkouts. An independent review on the exact code verified that charge amounts come only from server-side prices.
Measure
Checkout start to submitted payment to paid, by flow and locale, read on real purchases only.

Monetization · B2B
Show the team price

Shipped · 30-day readout
Saw
Every AI-cited answer about training cost showed a price. Ours said “we send a quote.”
Changed
I made team pricing public at US$99 per seat per year (10+ seats) in 14 languages.
Measure
Team inquiries and team checkouts for 30 days after release, against the 30 days before.

Retention · pricing
Guardrails before the test, not after

Designed in
Saw
Small volume means a bad test can quietly cost real sales.
Changed
Every win-back send holds out 100 users. Every paywall test carries an automatic rollback if new-cohort checkout-to-paid drops below 15%.
Measure
The campaign is read against its holdout, never against a before-and-after average.

Measurement
Count real people

Shipped
Saw
About 60% of analytics “active users” were bots.
Changed

Measure

Operations

The agent system that runs the iterations

I built a multi-agent system to do the research, analysis, prototyping and experiment operations behind every card above. An orchestrator turns a goal into a written brief: the goal, what “done” means, the limits and what was already tried. Cloud workers each build in their own copy of the code. Nothing ships on the word of the agent that built it.

Scheduled loops run the experiment operations: experiment readouts on their dates, AI-answer citation checks, nightly translation into 13 more languages with parity and accent checks, and ops watchdogs. Every loop names a verifier other than itself. Money, outbound messages and deletions always stop for a human.

Example: a wrong number caught before it published

A worker drafted a guide page that quoted a statistic from a public source. The independent reviewer checked the source and found the worker had counted the wrong thing. The worker corrected the figure against an exact-phrase match of the source, re-ran every check and updated the change notes. Only then did the page merge (Nov 2, 2026).

What broke, and what I changed

  • Green logs, frozen data. An analytics collector ran on schedule for 18 days and wrote no rows. The verifier now checks the output data, not the log.
  • Workers refusing work. 2 of about 10 cloud workers refused a task as an unverified message. Every brief now carries a short provenance and rules block, and refusals dropped to 1 in the next 9.
  • Build cost. Preview builds were most of the hosting bill. Every brief now says commit locally, run checks locally and push once.
